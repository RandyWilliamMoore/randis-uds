Wormhole: exotic matter to hold a throat open


Visser thin-shell wormhole made from flat space: surface mass density sigma = -c^2/(2 pi G a), so total shell mass M = -2 a c^2 / G. For a = 1 m that is about -2.7e27 kg (~ -1.4 Jupiter masses).

Travel: tidal forces at the throat




Morris-Thorne zero-redshift wormhole b(r) = b0^2/r: radial tide is zero; lateral tide = (gamma^2 v^2 / 2 r^2) |b' - b/r| L, which at the throat is gamma^2 v^2 L / b0^2. Solved for the fastest crossing speed that stays under the limit.

Casimir: real negative energy vs what a design needs



Ideal parallel plates: energy per area E/A = -pi^2 hbar c /(720 d^3). Real metals give less. Below ~1 nm the formula breaks down (plates are made of atoms).

Warp bubble: Alcubierre energy (Pfenning-Ford estimate)




E = -(1/12) (v/c)^2 (R^2/Delta) c^4/G. Quantum inequalities force the wall to be ~100 Planck lengths thin, which is what makes the number enormous (~1e62 kg at v=c, R=100 m).

Quantum inequality checker (Ford-Roman)



Bound (massless scalar, Lorentzian sampling time tau): rho >= -3 hbar /(32 pi^2 c^3 tau^4). Here tau = shell thickness / c, the light-crossing time of the region. A rough check, not a proof; the real result depends on the fields used.
